# Fenora — the exposure plan

A strategy for taking **fenora.pro** from zero to a feed that the UK window trade
actually follows — and the empty scheduling desk to run it on. The desk ships with
no posts: this document is the brief, you write the content.

---

## 1. The strategic problem, stated honestly

You have an extremely strong product and almost no audience. That is the easy part.

The hard part is that **"full stack business software for the window trade" is the
single least engaging sentence in your industry.** Nobody scrolling Instagram at
11pm on a Tuesday is excited about a configurator. But a fitter scrolling at 11pm on
a Tuesday will absolutely engage with:

> *"the reveal said 70. the reveal measured 52. the reveal is 70 at the top and 52 at
> the bottom and 61 in the middle, because of course it is."*

That is the whole strategy in one paragraph. **You are not a software company posting
about software. You are a trade account with a software punchline.** The product is
where the posts point; it is never what the post is about.

### The three-layer funnel

Almost every trade account that works runs the same three layers, usually without
realising it. Most accounts only do layer one and wonder why nothing converts.

| Layer | Who it's for | Share of posts | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Peers** | fitters, surveyors, joiners, builders | **70%** | Builds the follow. Creates the "this account gets it" feeling. |
| **Buyers** | MDs, owners, ops managers, surveyors | **25%** | Creates the DM. The person with a budget who follows the trade accounts. |
| **Product** | everyone, thinly | **5%** | Converts the buyer. Ten posts in three months is plenty. |

This is why the pillar mix aims for roughly 44% trade pain and 22% contrarian. That
ratio is deliberate — set it in **Settings → Pillar mix** and the planner will hold
it. A feed that is 60% product will never build an audience, and a feed with 0%
product will never make a sale.

### The number that decides everything

Instagram shows a post to roughly **10–30% of your followers, then ~5–10% of those
to non-followers** — but a Reel gets pushed far harder, and it is the only format
with a real shot at reaching someone who has never heard of you.

So: **statics build the feed, Reels reach the strangers.** You need both, and you
need the Reels.

## 2. Cadence — the bit that actually matters

Consistency beats brilliance. Every single time.

| Format | Per week | When | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Static (text card) | 3 | Mon / Wed / Fri, 08:15 | The daily digest beat. Fitters scroll before work. |
| Reel | 2–3 | Tue & Fri, 18:30 | After work, on the sofa, phone in hand. Peak trade reach. |
| Carousel | 1 | Thu | The "look how much I know" format. Highest save rate. |
| Story | daily | Any | Low effort, keeps you top-of-mind. Poll stickers. |
| **Commenting** | **20 min daily** | — | **The half of the strategy that isn't your posts.** |

**5 posts a week, every week, for 12 weeks.** Not 20 posts one week and none for a
month. The algorithm punishes absence far harder than it rewards volume, and — this
is the part people get wrong — **it punishes you for not showing up, not for showing
up badly.** A mediocre post on schedule beats a genius post in three weeks.

The dashboard's auto-scheduler does this for you. One click fills 90 days.

## 3. The commenting play (you were right to ask)

This is genuinely half the strategy, and most people are surprised by that.

**Why it works:** when you comment on a video, you are not posting to your followers
of 200. You are appearing in front of *that creator's* engaged audience, in a context
where they've already decided to be interested. It is the cheapest reach in social
media and almost nobody does it properly.

**The daily 20 minutes:**

1. **0–4 min — Search.** Ten search terms, in this order: `window fitter`,
   `double glazing fail`, `cowboy builder caught`, `trades life`, `plasterer`,
   `joiner`, `survey fail`, `conservation area`, `kitchen extension fail`,
   `build in public`. Plus every trades account in your own postcode.
2. **4–10 min — Watch properly.** 20–30 seconds of each. Do not skip in under 2
   seconds. Your skip rate drives your whole feed.
3. **10–18 min — Eight comments.** On the *specific thing in that video*, not
   "nice one". The good ones add a fact the creator didn't mention — that's what gets
   you a follow. 24 templates are in the Engagement tab, click to copy.
4. **18–20 min — Log it.** Tick the counter. The discipline is the point.

**The one rule: never pitch. Ever.** Not once, not "just at someone", not "in a
relevant context". The moment you comment "we do this too" or "DM me", you become an
ad, and the account dies. Add value, be funny, and let people find you. Some of the
best accounts in the trades have never once mentioned what they sell.

**The 30-day local play.** Every builder, plasterer and fitter within 20 miles. Comment
on *their* work properly, specifically, for 30 days. On a good installation, say
something only someone who installs windows would notice. You will get tagged, you
will get local followers, and local followers are the only followers who convert.

## 4. Platform roles — they are not the same job

Posting the identical thing to all three wastes two-thirds of each impression. The
dashboard rewrites the copy per platform automatically, but this is the thinking
behind it.

### Instagram — the engine
Where the trade lives. Reels and text cards. The first line of a caption is the only
line most people read, so the hook does double duty: it is the image text *and* the
caption opener. 18–22 hashtags, rotated, sitting at the bottom where they don't hurt
reading. Post 08:15 on weekdays — fitters check their phone before 7am and between
jobs.

### Facebook — where the owners actually are
MDs and owners in this trade are on Facebook, not Instagram, and the algorithm there
is far more forgiving of text posts. **No hashtags** — they read as spam. Longer,
more conversational, slightly more "here's a thought". This is the platform where a
PM or a surveyor's business case actually gets read.

### LinkedIn — credibility, not reach
Your audience here is about 40 people: window company owners, ops directors, maybe
some facilities and developers. That is not vanity — one LinkedIn DM from a
30-person window company paying £X a year is worth more than 10,000 likes.

LinkedIn rewards long-form and punishes engagement-bait. The same joke that wins on
Instagram will get 12 likes and zero DMs on LinkedIn. So: **argument first, anecdote
second.** Write a handful of LinkedIn-first long-form posts — a real argument, three
short paragraphs, no hashtags — and use them as the template for the rest.

## 5. The image problem, and why you mostly don't have one

There is no image pipeline in this repo, and that is deliberate. Artwork is yours to
make — shoot it on site, design it, or generate it — and the desk keeps an **image
brief** field per post plus the public URL Instagram needs to publish it.

Be honest about the economics: generating an image for every post takes weeks and
looks generic. The trade responds to **relatable and funny**, and text does that
faster than photography.

**So the primary visual should be the caption card** — the hook, big, on a dark
background in the brand colours. Write for it: short hook, no dependent clause, and
it has to work at thumbnail size.

> **28% of a standard UK casement is not glass.**
> **The cheapest quote is the job missing its most expensive part.**
> **A keystone is two seconds of work and the whole job.**

Aim for roughly half your posts to land that way. Save real photography for the ones
where a picture genuinely beats text — the reveal, the dog, the cavity full of foam.

Five house styles are kept in `content/tags.json` (`styles`) for exactly that: a
photoreal editorial look, a candid trades-at-work look, a broadsheet-cartoon
illustration, a product screen mock and a plain typographic card background. Append
one to every brief you generate from — the style suffix is what makes twenty
separately-generated images look like one campaign rather than a ransom note.

**Batch tip:** make a week of artwork in one sitting, same style suffix, same
colours. Then paste the public URLs into the posts and schedule the lot.

## 6. The first 30 days, day by day

**Days 1–3 — Set up, don't post yet.**
Switch to an Instagram Professional (Business) account. Link it to a Facebook Page.
Create a LinkedIn Page for the company, not your personal profile. Bio: one line about
the trade, not the software. *"We build the software the window trade wishes it had.
Also: opinions about reveals."* Add a link to fenora.pro. Do not run a giveaway.

**Days 4–14 — Post 5× a week, comment 20 min a day. That's it.**
No ads, no giveaway, no launch post. Run the auto-scheduler, work down the calendar,
and spend the commenting time properly. Expect almost nothing for ten days. That is
normal and it is where everyone quits.

**Days 15–30 — Read the signals.**
Look at which posts got saves and which got shares (shares >> likes for B2B, always).
Look at who followed you — if they are fitters rather than homeowners, the strategy
is right. Double down on the pillar that performed. Start replying to every single
comment within a few hours; a fast reply is one of the strongest ranking signals
there is. By day 30 you should have your first 50 non-random followers and your first
couple of DMs from real window companies.

**Days 31–90 — Layer in the sales content.**
By now the account has a point of view. Introduce the job finder properly. Introduce
the configurator. Introduce the "we don't publish testimonials" position. These
conversations land completely differently on an account with 2,000 relevant followers
than on an empty one. Do not skip this ramp — the single most common mistake in
trade-account marketing is jumping straight to selling.

## 7. What to actually measure

Ignore follower count daily. It is the wrong number and it will make you
unhappy.

| Metric | Why | Target at 90 days |
|---|---|---|
| **Saves + shares per post** | Real signal of "this is useful", not "this is pretty" | 5+ |
| **Comments from fitters** | Proves you're in the right room | 2–5/post |
| **DMs from window businesses** | The only number that pays rent | 3–5/week by month 3 |
| **Profile visits from non-followers** | Reach leaking to interest | Growing steadily |
| **Posts shipped** | The only one fully in your control | **260+ in 90 days** |

Run a simple UTM on your bio link (`fenora.pro/?src=ig`) so you can see traffic by
platform once you have any.

## 8. The honest part: what won't work

- **Giveaways.** "Win a free set of windows" attracts homeowners and prize hunters.
  It will cost you the trade audience and take weeks to undo.
- **Competitor callouts.** Named or unnamed, it reads as insecure and the trade is
  small. Every fitter knows every other fitter.
- **Boosting posts.** £50 boosting a joke gets you £50 of likes from strangers who
  don't install windows. Put the same money into a trade newsletter sponsorship
  instead, if you ever do spend.
- **Generic AI imagery of smiling people pointing at laptops.** It is the visual
  language of every B2B SaaS account on earth. The trade scrolls past it. Real
  workshops, real reveals, real foam.
- **Posting only when you have news.** You have news roughly twice a year. The feed
  has to run on relatability, not announcements.

## 9. And when someone asks what Fenora is

You will get the DM. Here's the answer, and it works because it's short:

> "All of it, in one place — CRM, planning-job search, the survey app, the window
> configurator, quoting, production, tasks and deliveries. One job record from
> enquiry to final invoice, so nothing gets retyped and nothing falls between two
> systems. Prices are published on the site. Try it on your own product range —
> it's priced with your rates, not a sample dataset."

Do not demo. Do not send a brochure. Offer them their own range in their own pricing,
because *"what does it cost for me, with my glass prices?"* is the question that
closes this sale every single time.

## 10. Where the files are

```
fenora-content-desk/
├── STRATEGY.md              ← this
├── README.md                ← how to run the tool
├── server.js                ← local server + platform publishing
├── config.example.json      ← copy to config.json and add your tokens
├── app/                     ← the Content Desk (index.html, app.js, styles.css)
└── content/
    ├── build.py             ← compiles posts_*.py → posts.json + posts.csv
    ├── posts.json           ← the frame: pillars, tagsets, banner + 0 posts
    ├── tags.json            ← hashtag sets, discovery tags, house image styles
    ├── posts_*.py           ← optional: bulk content, one file per pillar group
    └── schedule.json        ← your dates, ticks and posts (created on first save)
```

Posts you write in the dashboard live in the browser and in `schedule.json` —
they never need `build.py`. Use the Python part files only when you have a pile
of copy to import at once.
